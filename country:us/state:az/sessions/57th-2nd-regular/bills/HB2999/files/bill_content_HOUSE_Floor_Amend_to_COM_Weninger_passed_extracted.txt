FLOOR AMENDMENT EXPLANATION
57th Legislature, 2nd Regular Session
Majority Research Staff
HB 2999: municipal improvement districts; technical correction
WENINGER FLOOR AMENDMENT
1. Replace infrastructure finance district with state housing affordability district.
2. Modifies the definition of ad valorem and municipality.
3. Requires any land within the district that is located within the adopted
planning area of a municipality be subject to a current written annexation or
pre-annexation agreement between the municipality and the owners of that
land.
4. Makes clarifying changes.
5. Adds that the notice of the filing of the petition to state that the municipality
or the county, as applicable, may submit to the Authority within thirty days
after the filing of the petition a written notice that identifies a basis for denial
of the petition.
6. Changes the criteria for denying a petition by finding that the financial
information submitted in the petition illustrates that the district will not have,
rather than has or will have, the financial ability to discharge its proposed
indebtedness.
7. Includes, as a criterion for denying a petition, that the formation of the
proposed district will materially violate any existing and applicable
development agreement with the municipality or the county that relates to
public infrastructure and that is recorded against the land included within the
boundaries of the district with respect to the design, construction, conveyance,
operation or maintenance of the public infrastructure.
8. Specifies any action of the Authority or the executive director is the exercise of
an administrative function involving the determination of fundamental
governmental policy as prescribed in statute.
9. Includes requirements relating to notification of board vacancy.
10. Prohibits the board from levying an o/m tax if no boards or obligations remain
outstanding.
11. Instructs the District to include, on its website, a description of the public
infrastructure the District is authorized to finance, identifying which
categories of public infrastructure are required by the applicable municipality

12. Requires a District to be dissolved automatically and without any action from
the board or the qualified electors in the year that is five years after completion
of all work to be performed under the District’s general plan, as long as the
authority has verified that no bonds or obligations remain outstanding.
13. Stipulates the District must be dissolved in the year after the bonds or
obligations are paid in full if any bonds or obligations remain outstanding.
14. Includes requirements relating to:
a. zoning and land use authority; and
b. financing, constructing or acquiring public infrastructure.
15. Specifies District requirements and regulations do not authorize the District
to pledge the full faith and credit or the taxing power of a municipality or
county and any bonds or other obligations of a district are obligations only of
the district and are payable only from the sources that are pledged for their
payment.

30 Sec. 6. Legislative findings; purpose
31 A. The legislature finds and declares that:
32 1. The provision of public infrastructure in advance of development
33 is essential to housing affordability, economic development and orderly
34 growth.
35 2. State housing affordability districts provide a uniform,
36 statewide mechanism for financing public infrastructure in a manner that:
37 (a) Protects taxpayers through limited tax rates and debt
38 limitations.
39 (b) Ensures transparency and public accountability.
40 (c) Facilitates early delivery of public infrastructure at lower
41 cost.
42 3. The Arizona finance authority is the appropriate statewide
43 certification authority to ensure uniform financial, engineering, and
44 statutory compliance for such districts.
45 4. The certification and oversight functions provided in this
46 chapter constitute governmental functions serving valid public purposes.
47 B. The legislature further finds and declares that:

1 1. State housing affordability districts exercise limited, special-
2 purpose governmental powers relating solely to the financing,
3 construction, operation and maintenance of public infrastructure.
4 2. The financial obligations of a district, including ad valorem
5 taxes, assessments, and liens, are borne directly and proportionally by
6 real property within the district.
7 3. Because the powers and financial burdens of a district
8 disproportionately affect real property owners, it is reasonable and
9 constitutionally permissible to allocate voting power in district
10 elections based on land ownership and acreage.
11 4. Elections conducted pursuant to this chapter are fiscal
