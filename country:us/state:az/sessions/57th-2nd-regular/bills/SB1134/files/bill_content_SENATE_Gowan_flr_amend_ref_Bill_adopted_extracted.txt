4 16-1019. Political signs; printed materials; tampering;
5 violation; classification; definitions
6 A. It is a class 2 misdemeanor for any person to knowingly remove,
7 alter, deface or cover any political sign of any candidate for public
8 office or in support of or opposition to any ballot measure, question or
9 issue or knowingly remove, alter or deface any political mailers,
10 handouts, flyers or other printed materials of a candidate or in support
11 of or opposition to any ballot measure, question or issue that are
12 delivered by hand to a residence for the period commencing seventy-one
13 days before THE FIRST DAY THAT EARLY BALLOTS MAY BE MAILED FOR an election
14 AS PRESCRIBED BY SECTION 16-542 and ending fifteen days after the
15 election, except that for a sign for a candidate in a primary election who
16 advances to the general election, the period ends fifteen days after the
17 general election. For a sign that supports or opposes a ballot measure,
18 question or issue, this subsection applies only for the election at which
19 the ballot measure, question or issue is scheduled to appear on the ballot
20 and for the period commencing seventy-one days before that election and
21 ending fifteen days after that election.
22 B. This section does not apply to the removal, alteration, defacing
23 or covering of a political sign or other printed materials by the
24 candidate or the authorized agent of the candidate in support of whose
25 election the sign or materials were placed, by a person authorized by the
26 committee in support of or opposition to a ballot measure, question or
27 issue that provided the sign or printed materials, by the owner or
28 authorized agent of the owner of private property on which such signs or

4 C. Notwithstanding any other statute, ordinance or regulation, a
5 city, town or county of this state shall not remove, alter, deface or
6 cover any political sign if the following conditions are met:
7 1. The sign is placed in a public right-of-way that is owned or
8 controlled by that jurisdiction.
9 2. The sign supports or opposes a candidate for public office or it
10 supports or opposes a ballot measure, question or issue.
11 3. The sign is not placed in a location that is hazardous to public
12 safety, obstructs clear vision in the area or interferes with the
13 requirements of the Americans with disabilities act (42 United States Code
14 sections 12101 through 12213 and 47 United States Code sections 225 and
15 611).
16 4. The sign has a maximum area of sixteen square feet, if the sign
17 is located in an area zoned for residential use, or a maximum area of
18 thirty-two square feet if the sign is located in any other area.
19 5. The sign contains the name and telephone number or website
20 address of the candidate or campaign committee contact person.
21 D. If the city, town or county deems that the placement of a
22 political sign constitutes an emergency, the jurisdiction may immediately
23 relocate the sign. The jurisdiction shall notify the candidate or
24 campaign committee that placed the sign within twenty-four hours after the
25 relocation. If a sign is placed in violation of subsection C of this
26 section and the placement is not deemed to constitute an emergency, the
27 city, town or county may notify the candidate or campaign committee that
28 placed the sign of the violation. If the sign remains in violation at
29 least twenty-four hours after the jurisdiction notified the candidate or
30 campaign committee, the jurisdiction may remove the sign. The
31 jurisdiction shall contact the candidate or campaign committee contact and
32 shall retain the sign for at least ten business days to allow the
33 candidate or campaign committee to retrieve the sign without penalty.
34 E. A city, town or county employee acting within the scope of the
35 employee's employment is not liable for an injury caused by the failure to
36 remove a sign pursuant to subsection D of this section unless the employee
37 intended to cause injury or was grossly negligent.
38 F. Subsection C of this section does not apply to commercial
39 tourism, commercial resort and hotel sign free zones as those zones are
40 designated by municipalities. The total area of those zones shall not be
41 larger than three square miles, and each zone shall be identified as a
42 specific contiguous area where, by resolution of the municipal governing
43 body, the municipality has determined that based on a predominance of
44 commercial tourism, resort and hotel uses within the zone the placement of
45 political signs within the rights-of-way in the zone will detract from the
46 scenic and aesthetic appeal of the area within the zone and deter its
