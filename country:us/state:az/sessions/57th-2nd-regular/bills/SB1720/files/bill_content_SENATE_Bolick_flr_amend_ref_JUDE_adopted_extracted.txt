Bill Number: S.B. 1720
Bolick Floor Amendment
Reference to: JUDICIARY AND ELECTIONS
Committee Amendment
Amendment drafted by: Leg. Council
FLOOR AMENDMENT EXPLANATION
1. Modifies the prescribed relevant factors to a child's physical and emotional well-being to include
the child's attachment relationships, developmental stage and developmental needs, including the
need for stability, continuity of care and emotional security.
2. Requires the court, when determining that equal parenting time is not in the child's best interests,
to consider the child's age, developmental stage, attachment needs and ability to tolerate frequent
transitions between households.
3. Requires the court to prioritize stability, continuity of care and the child's emotional security, for
decisions involving children of a young age or those with developmental needs.
4. Outlines conditions which prohibit a presumption of equal parenting time, including conditions
relating to orders of protection and domestic violence, and conditions in which the court finds that
equal parenting time would endanger the child.
5. Allows the court to consider the child's wishes in approving a parental agreement, if the child is of
suitable age and maturity.
6. Prohibits the court from ordering equal parenting time over the objection of a child unless the court
makes specific findings that the order will not endanger the child.
7. Requires the court to make a specific finding that both parents are safe and do not pose a risk of
harm to the child before applying a presumption of equal parenting time.
8. Removes the requirement that the court consider newly established criteria relating to equal
parenting time presumptions when awarding temporary legal decision-making or parenting time.

6 Sec. 6. Legislative findings
7 The legislature finds:
8 1. That the public policy of this state is to promote the best
9 interests of the child by encouraging frequent, meaningful and continuing
10 contact with both parents following a legal separation or dissolution of
11 marriage.
12 2. That children benefit emotionally, academically and
13 developmentally when both parents are significantly involved in the
14 child's life.
15 3. That equal parenting time reduces conflict, fosters stability
16 and safeguards the fundamental rights of both the child and the child's
17 parents.
18 4. That a rebuttable presumption that equal parenting is in the
19 best interests of the child reduces unnecessary litigation and protects
20 children from adversarial custody disputes.
